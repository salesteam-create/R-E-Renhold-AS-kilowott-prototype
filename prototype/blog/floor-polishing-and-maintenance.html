Floor polishing and maintenance: how to make floors last longer

FloorsPublished 27 November 20263 min read

The floor is the surface in a building that takes the most wear. With the right daily cleaning and a new protective polish when needed, a floor covering can stay looking good for many more years. Here is what you need to know.

What is floor polishing?

Floor polishing means giving the floor a thin, protective layer of polish. The polish takes the wear from shoes, chairs and trolleys instead of the floor covering itself. Over time the polish gets dirty and scratched. The old layer is then removed by stripping, before the floor gets a new coat.

Which floors can be polished?

Vinyl and linoleum

These are the most common floors to polish, and they are found in most offices, schools, shops and shared areas. Linoleum does not tolerate strongly alkaline products, so use pH-neutral cleaners day to day.

Tiles

Tiles are not usually polished. Here, maintenance is about thorough cleaning, clean grout and descaling in wet rooms.

Wood and parquet

Wooden floors are either oiled or lacquered and are maintained with their own products, such as soap or oil. They should not be polished.

Signs your floor needs a new coat

  • The floor looks dull and grey, even straight after cleaning.
  • You can see clear walkways where people walk most.
  • Scratches and stains stick and are hard to remove.
  • Dirt seems to cling faster than before.

How often should floors be polished?

It depends on the traffic. Entrances, corridors and busy shops need it more often than a rarely used meeting room. Many do it once or twice a year in busy areas, and less often elsewhere. Good daily cleaning means longer gaps between treatments.

Daily maintenance that extends the floor's life

  • Good entrance mats stop grit and water before they reach the floor.
  • Dry mop or vacuum before wet cleaning, so you do not drag grit around.
  • The right dosage. Too much detergent leaves a sticky film that attracts dirt.
  • A damp, not wet, mop. Too much water can get into joints and edges.
  • Felt pads under chairs and table legs mean fewer scratches.
  • Remove stains quickly, before they set.

How stripping and polishing works

  1. Furniture is moved and the floor is vacuumed.
  2. The old polish is stripped off with a machine and a suitable product.
  3. The floor is rinsed and left to dry completely.
  4. New polish is applied in several thin coats, with drying time between each.
  5. Furniture goes back once the floor has cured.

Frequently asked questions

How long before the floor can be used again?

It depends on the product and the number of coats. The floor must dry completely between coats, so many choose to have the work done in the evening or at the weekend.

Can parquet be polished?

No. Standard floor polish is made for coverings like vinyl and linoleum. Parquet and wooden floors are maintained with soap, oil or lacquer, depending on how they have been treated.

How often should floors be polished?

In high-traffic areas, once or twice a year is common. Rooms with little use can manage with less frequent treatment.
